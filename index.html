What vision–language–action models fail to ground

2026 · BOMB: a Board Manipulation Benchmark · write-up · code

Current VLA benchmarks reward coarse semantic manipulation — pick the red block, open the drawer — and so overstate how well these policies handle the fine spatial grounding real tasks need. We built a simulated Go-stone placement benchmark, where the policy must put a stone on a board coordinate named in language, and evaluated OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, SpatialVLA and π0 on it.

Beyond success rates, we ran intervention-based explainability: masking image patches and text tokens to find which inputs the policy actually consumes, and activation mapping to localise where the coordinate is processed. The dominant failure mode is specific — models track the stone and the gripper adequately, but do not robustly ground a language-specified coordinate to a placement location.

  • 34% success for OpenVLA without perturbation
  • 0% for every model under visual distribution shift — lighting, camera pose, object arrangement
  • offline metrics stayed reasonable throughout the collapse

Reproducing physics-informed diffusion models

2026 · after Bastek, Sun & Kochmann, ICLR 2025 · write-up · code

Whether a diffusion model can carry a PDE constraint in its training objective, rather than being corrected after the fact. We replicated all five variants — plain diffusion, PG-Diffusion, CoCoGen, PIDM-ME, PIDM-SE — on Darcy flow and topology optimisation, and added Sliced Wasserstein Distance alongside residual error and test loss, because a model can drive its residual down while its sample distribution drifts.

The reproduction held, and the ablations found the method sensitive to residual-loss weighting and to the training noise schedule. Reduced-precision training turned out to act as a regulariser rather than a cost.

  • residuals matched the paper to within 3%
  • Darcy residual error 1.2 × 10−2 → 5.5 × 10−3 (≈56%) from hyperparameter search
  • training wall-clock 13 h → 44 min — hardware selection, compiled kernels, bf16

An image processing pipeline for satellite and aerial imagery

2026 · Group 13, Applied Image Processing, TU Delft · with A. Kheskani, C. Scorția, V. Petkov

An end-to-end enhancement pipeline over AVIRIS-NG hyperspectral surveys (425 bands, 390–2510 nm, 5.8 m sampling) and USGS LiDAR. My contribution covered hyperspectral preprocessing and the multispectral indices — NDVI, NDRE, HDWI, TCARI/OSAVI — a hyperspectral zero-shot single-image super-resolution model, and mineral identification by spectral angle against the USGS library.

Zero-shot super-resolution needed reworking for spectral data: 3D convolutions in place of 2D, trilinear interpolation across the cube instead of per-band bicubic, and an ensemble L1 + Spectral Angle Mapper loss to keep each pixel's spectral response intact under upscaling. The rest of the group contributed stitching, dual-branch cloud removal, SAM-guided object editing, U-Net segmentation and LiDAR–bathymetry depth fusion.

  • ZSSR 25.57 dB vs 24.23 dB for trilinear interpolation
  • ~1000× speedup on spectral angle matching via multithreaded batch comparison
  • dual-branch cloud removal 28.9 dB / 0.87 SSIM on SEN12MS-CR, over 27.1 / 0.84 single-branch

A control dataset for non-local transition structure

2026 · code & write-up

A gridworld with walls and wormholes, built as a control dataset for the original DQN result. The question it isolates: does a convolutional value predictor learn that two distant cells are one step apart, or does it fall back on spatial proximity? The experiment trains on small wormhole gaps and tests on large ones, against a wormhole-ablated control input and naive distance baselines — then repeats on harder maps with randomised positions and decoy wormholes.

Data and computational efficiency in temporal action localization

2023 · BSc thesis, TU Delft Computer Vision Lab · advised by A. Lengyel and O. Strafforello · code

Temporal action localization models are compared on full datasets and unlimited compute, which is not the regime most people train them in. We measured data efficiency by training on progressively reduced datasets and computational efficiency across video lengths, and derived development practices from the result: TemporalMaxer holds up best when data is scarce and is cheapest at inference, TriDet when training time is the binding constraint.
