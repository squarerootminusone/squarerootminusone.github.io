Hyperspectral satellite mineral deposit classification

December 2025 – January 2026 · Applied Image Processing, TU Delft · with A. Kheskani, C. Scorția and V. Petkov

An end-to-end enhancement pipeline over AVIRIS-NG hyperspectral surveys — 425 bands, 390–2510 nm, 5.8 m spatial sampling — together with USGS LiDAR. My contribution covered hyperspectral preprocessing and the multispectral indices, a hyperspectral zero-shot single-image super-resolution model, and mineral identification by spectral angle against the USGS spectral library.

My contribution

Hyperspectral preprocessing and indices

Loading and preprocessing the cubes, removing noisy mid-infrared bands corrupted by atmospheric moisture, normalising out artifact outliers, and producing a true-colour approximation with tunable gamma and saturation. Four vegetation and water indices: NDVI, NDRE, HDWI and TCARI/OSAVI. HDWI works outside the noisy SWIR bands around 1450 nm and in testing gave markedly more precise water-body delineation than the standard NDWI.

Zero-shot single-image super-resolution

Zero-shot super-resolution needed reworking for spectral data. 3D convolutions in place of the original 2D; trilinear interpolation across the whole cube rather than per-band bicubic, so cross-band correlation survives; and an ensemble L1 + Spectral Angle Mapper loss to keep each pixel's spectral response intact under upscaling. Training uses a multiscale pyramid on repeatedly downsampled low/high-resolution pairs.

Mineral identification

Comparing preprocessed imagery against laboratory spectra from the USGS library by Spectral Angle Mapper, with vegetation and water masked out first using the indices above. Naively this is O(nmat · nbands · w · h); a multithreaded batch matrix implementation makes it tractable on full scenes.

Results

  • ZSSR 25.57 dB against 24.23 dB for trilinear interpolation
  • ~1000× speedup on spectral angle matching for large images
  • Dual-branch cloud removal 28.9 dB / 0.87 SSIM on SEN12MS-CR, over 27.1 / 0.84 for the best single-branch baseline

The rest of the pipeline

Contributed by the other three: hyperspectral image stitching with spectral integrity preserved; a dual-branch cloud removal architecture routing between NAFNet for thin cloud and SwinIR for thick cloud by predicted opacity; SAM-guided object removal and insertion with DiffusionSat inpainting and Poisson blending; U-Net semantic segmentation with recolouring and saliency operations; and depth extraction fusing LiDAR canopy height models with spectrally derived bathymetry.

A known limitation: without on-site reference spectra the mineral matching cannot resolve mixtures.
